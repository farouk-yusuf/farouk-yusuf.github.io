Requirements and scope

Simulated workplace brief

Ahead of an annual board review, the Director of Customer Experience needs a clear account of tenant satisfaction: how Platform compares with similar landlords, where services fall behind and which changes need investigation before the report goes to the board.

I structured the brief around the decision, comparison group, measures and evidence needed. For this portfolio scenario, the resulting scope is an annual benchmark and a prioritised investigation plan; it is not an evaluation of individual tenants or a costed service redesign.

Requirements summary
Business problem
An overall satisfaction score can conceal service weaknesses, while changes in recording can distort the annual performance report.
Core questions
How does Platform compare with similar landlords? Which services warrant attention? Are large changes comparable across years? Do escalated complaints support the same priorities?
Business relevance
Give the board a defensible account of performance and help customer-experience, repairs and neighbourhood teams choose the next investigations.
Defined scope
Published 2023/24 and 2024/25 Tenant Satisfaction Measures, plus a separate comparison of Housing Ombudsman reports labelled 2025/26. No tenant-level records, intervention costs or implemented outcomes.

03

A relevant peer group changes the interpretation

I selected large housing associations as the main comparator rather than treating every Midlands landlord as equivalent. Platform’s 64.6% overall satisfaction matches the large-landlord median; the broader Midlands housing-association median is 77.4%. The choice changes the comparison materially, so the cohort must be visible.

Comparability check

Platform reported 2,316 survey responses and predominantly used telephone interviews. Telephone was also the most common method across the published dataset. The primary cohort was not restricted to telephone surveys: method, tenant mix and response differences can still affect comparisons.

04

The overall score conceals specific service gaps

Six of the twelve tenant-satisfaction measures sit in the bottom quarter of the large-landlord cohort. Communal areas show the largest shortfall: 55.4% satisfied against a 64.5% peer median. Communication, listening and neighbourhood services also warrant attention.

Performance is mixed. Repairs-service satisfaction is above the peer median, as is satisfaction with home safety. Separately, operational returns record 100% completion for five building-safety checks and 98.2% of emergency repairs within target. Compliance and tenant perceptions answer different questions; neither should substitute for the other.

Listening, communication and maintenance are starting priorities

I combined the size of Platform’s gaps with the way service scores move together across landlords. The strongest association with overall satisfaction concerns well-maintained homes. Listening and communication also combine meaningful shortfalls with strong associations, making them sensible starting points for a service review.

Statistical interpretation

A correlation near 1 means landlord-level scores tend to move together. It does not show how an individual tenant would respond, establish causation or estimate the benefit of an intervention. Tenant mix and other unmeasured differences may contribute to these associations.

Communal areas still need a local review because the shortfall is the largest. Fair treatment and repair time also meet the screening rule. Final priorities would depend on tenant feedback, urgency, cost and service capacity; those inputs are unavailable here.

Association method and full screening results

The script uses complete landlord rows across all twelve survey measures, calculates Pearson correlations and flags measures with a correlation of at least 0.80 and a negative Platform gap. Five measures meet that screening rule. The three recommendations above are an analyst-selected starting set, not an automatic or cost-optimised ranking.

drv = cur[list(TP)].dropna()
corr = drv.corr()["TP01"].drop("TP01").sort_values(ascending=False)

06

Repair counts need reconciliation before a performance conclusion

I checked the underlying counts before interpreting the decline. The rate fell by 29.4 percentage points, while the repairs counted fell from 90,250 to 13,872. This combination raises a comparability question; it does not prove a recording error or rule out a real deterioration.

Data validation and escalation

Request job-level totals by year, repair type, completion status and inclusion rule from Repairs. Build a bridge from the earlier population to the later one, confirm the 28-day definition and then quantify any remaining performance change.

The same review identified large increases in recorded anti-social behaviour cases and stage-one complaints. Higher recorded volumes can reflect worsening experience, broader recording or both; the service owners need to establish which explanation fits.

Complaint findings reinforce the need to investigate repairs

I examined Ombudsman findings as a second source of service evidence. These concern escalated complaints, not a representative sample of tenants, and cover reports labelled 2025/26 rather than the 2024/25 satisfaction return. They provide context, not a direct validation of the survey result.

Reconciliation outstanding

The extraction records a 38% headline rate and a 56.5% median for the other 20 landlords. Its counts do not reproduce Platform’s rate, so I would hold the rate and ranking from a formal board conclusion until verified against the original report. The category analysis remains provisional pending that check.

Scope and interpretation limits

Only published aggregates are available. Survey method and tenant mix are not controlled, year-on-year respondents may differ, and correlation is across landlords. The TSM and Ombudsman cohorts and periods differ. Repair definitions and the Ombudsman rate discrepancy require follow-up. Raw inputs are not committed, so website checks verify existing outputs rather than rerunning or independently verifying the original source analysis.

Power BI · planned reporting view

A planned Power BI view would show satisfaction and peer gaps by measure, with year and comparator-group filters. It should keep survey results, operational performance and Ombudsman outcomes on separate views, display cohort sizes and flag unresolved comparisons. Tenant-group or estate filters would require internal data; no dashboard or implemented service improvement is claimed.
