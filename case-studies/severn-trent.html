Independent public-data case study

Fewer spills. A drier year. Progress to examine.

Reported spills fell 41%. I checked rainfall and monitor reliability before identifying the sites that still warrant operational review.

The decision this analysis supports.

The performance team needs a defensible account of the annual change and clear exception lists for maintenance and investment. I combined six years of Environment Agency returns, normalised changing file formats and checked whether falling spill totals reflected weather, monitoring or a change in performance.

What a closer look revealed.

Spills fell from 62,085 to 36,471 while Midlands rainfall fell from about 1,000 mm to 717 mm. A simple model attributes roughly half of the reduction to rainfall, but that estimate is indicative. A useful cross-check is 2022: rainfall was similar to 2025, and the average spills per reliably monitored overflow were about 23% lower in 2025 than in 2022.

My recommendation

Report spills alongside rainfall and monitor coverage. Investigate the remaining high-spilling sites, beginning with those without a reported improvement action. Similar annual rainfall is a useful comparison, but differences in storm intensity, local conditions and asset changes remain.

The average improvement leaves concentrated risks.

Seventy-six overflows still recorded at least 60 spills in 2025, including 58 in the Trent basin. Thirteen had no improvement action reported. Storm tanks at treatment works represent 13% of overflows but account for 47% of spill hours, making them a useful starting point for asset-level review.

How I built and checked it.

  1. Normalised monitor operation from fractions and percentages, and converted several duration formats into decimal hours.
  2. Matched changing overflow identifiers across years using the old-format ID field.
  3. Calculated trend averages using monitors operating for at least 90% of the year so missing observation time did not masquerade as better performance.
  4. Used spills per overflow as well as totals because the reporting population changes between years.
  5. Kept the rainfall model simple and compared similar-rainfall years as a separate sense check.

What I would ask the team to do next.

Maintenance

Prioritise the 27 monitors reported below 90% operation in both 2024 and 2025.

Investment and operations

Review the 13 high-spilling overflows without a reported improvement action, then investigate the wider high-spill group.

Reporting

Show rainfall, observation coverage and changes in the reporting population alongside annual spill totals.

Where the evidence stops.

  • Annual regional rainfall is a coarse proxy for event-level catchment conditions. Weather adjustment is not causal proof.
  • Reliably monitored groups can change composition between years; they are not automatically a fixed matched panel.
  • The management summary lists 87 low-uptime monitors in 2025; the yearly CSV lists 90. The site avoids presenting either as a reconciled total. The denominator needs checking before publication.
  • The 2021 overflow-level totals differ from the EA summary by eight spills and seven hours; this small discrepancy is recorded in the data-quality log.

What I learned

A favourable headline needs the same scrutiny as a poor one. Better monitor coverage also changes what is observed, so I made that part of the interpretation rather than treating it as background detail.
