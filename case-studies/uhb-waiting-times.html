March’s recovery is under pressure again.

The waiting list grew 7.5% after March 2026. Referrals rose while completed pathways stayed broadly flat, making the next milestone harder to reach.

The decision this analysis supports.

A monthly elective-care report should explain performance against milestones, emerging pressures and where to investigate next. I processed 41 monthly NHS England extracts for UHB, six large Midlands peer trusts and England. Counts refer to RTT pathways; they should not automatically be read as unique people.

What a closer look revealed.

UHB reached 68.6% within 18 weeks in March 2026, above the stated 65% milestone. By August it was 66.6%, while the list had grown from 89,544 to 96,303. In April–August, clock starts rose from 115,481 to 133,091; clock stops moved only from 103,997 to 104,806. This shows growing demand pressure alongside broadly flat completed activity. It does not establish that capacity is sufficient.

My recommendation

Review referrals and completed pathways together, with a specialty-level recovery plan. The analysis estimates around 1,550 additional clock stops a month to stabilise the list under its assumptions. That is a planning estimate to reconcile with the full stock-flow balance, rather than a guaranteed delivery target.

The most elaborate forecast was less accurate.

The demand-and-capacity model had 8.4% back-test error and the trend model 6.07%, compared with 2.5% for a no-change baseline. Both models missed the turn around March 2026. I would present the roughly 107,000 March 2027 list as a continuation scenario and refresh it monthly, with assumptions shown explicitly.

How I built and checked it.

  1. Read 41 monthly extracts and aggregated commissioner-level rows to trust and specialty level.
  2. Checked for missing submissions and unknown clock-start dates across the selected series.
  3. Caught an extract-schema problem: the Part 2 Total column was blank. Summed the weekly time bands and checked against Total All.
  4. Compared UHB with six other large Midlands trusts and England, keeping local and national comparisons visible.
  5. Back-tested a demand-and-capacity model and a trend model against a simple no-change baseline before presenting an outlook.

What I would ask the team to do next.

Elective performance

Refresh starts, stops and the list balance monthly, including removals and adjustments. Investigate the recent demand increase with commissioners.

Specialty leadership

Review ENT and Ophthalmology, which account for 110 of 175 remaining 52-week waits. Investigate Gynaecology’s 38% annual list growth and 45.9% 18-week performance.

Planning

Use the March 2027 continuation scenario alongside a baseline and capacity assumptions. Check milestones against current NHS planning guidance.

Where the evidence stops.

  • Clock stops include completed pathways such as treatments and discharges; they are not a direct measure of theatre productivity.
  • Starts minus stops alone does not fully reconcile the waiting-list stock. Other removals and reporting adjustments must be considered.
  • Size-based peers do not ensure identical specialty mix, local demand or patient complexity.
  • The models failed their back-test relative to the baseline. The outlook is a scenario, and the published milestones require checking against current planning guidance.

What I learned

Reporting that a simple baseline beat my models made the analysis more useful. A planning audience needs to understand the conditions under which an outlook might be wrong.
