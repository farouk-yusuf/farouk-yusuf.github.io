Requirements and reporting scope

Simulated workplace brief

During a monthly elective-performance meeting, I was asked to explain the latest waiting-time figures and identify services needing a closer review. I first established the reporting month, the pathway definitions and the decisions the report should support: monitoring recovery, investigating list growth and directing specialty-level follow-up.

I used my requirements framework to separate performance reporting from capacity planning. Published totals can identify pressure and guide questions; an operational recovery plan also needs appointment capacity, cancellations, pathway stages and the reasons records leave the list.

Requirements summary
Business problem
Waiting times improved substantially, but the list began growing again after March 2026. A strong position against selected peers could conceal emerging local pressure.
Core questions
Has recovery continued? Do starts and stops explain the list change? Where are long waits concentrated? Which specialties are deteriorating? Are the planning models useful?
Business relevance
Give operational leaders a clear performance account and a focused set of specialty, data-validation and planning questions.
Defined scope
Consultant-led Referral to Treatment data, April 2023–August 2026; UHB, six selected Midlands trusts and England. No patient-level records, appointment-capacity data, diagnostic waits, cancer pathways or emergency care.

Data preparation and validation

Referral to Treatment (RTT) tracks consultant-led elective pathways. One person may have more than one pathway, so the counts below are not unique patients. A clock start records a new pathway; a clock stop can reflect treatment or another qualifying end to the wait.

Inside the trust, I would request the patient tracking list and operational records through their owners. Here, I used public provider/specialty aggregates, preserving the distinction between reporting pressure and explaining its causes.

07

Model validation limits the planning outlook

I held out the final six observed months before evaluating the models, rather than assessing them only on the data used to build them. I compared them with a simple no-change baseline so additional complexity had to demonstrate value.

Back-test accuracy: lower average absolute percentage error is better
MethodMean absolute percentage errorInterpretation
No-change baseline2.50%Most accurate of the three methods on this test.
Six-month trend6.07%Continued the earlier decline and missed the turn.
Demand/capacity model8.40%Seasonal starts/stops and an average residual did not capture the turn.

Mean absolute percentage error averages the size of each monthly miss relative to the actual list. It measures accuracy on these six months, not a confidence level. A single holdout is limited evidence, but it gives a clear reason to avoid using the more complex model as a committed recovery forecast.

Continuation scenario, assumptions and model code

The original continuation model reuses last year’s seasonal starts and stops, scales each by the latest three-month year-on-year ratio and carries forward the recent average residual. It gives 107,190 waiting pathways in March 2027 in the rounded workbook. It has no prediction interval and failed to outperform the baseline in the held-out test.

Within this model, holding the list flat would require roughly 1,555 additional clock stops per month, with every other assumption unchanged. This is arithmetic from the scenario, not a feasible capacity plan or a count of treatment slots. It also depends on an unclassified residual continuing. The key-figures CSV truncates the final unrounded estimate to 107,189; the workbook rounds it to 107,190.

def dc_forecast(hist, h):
    out, wl_ = [], hist.waiting_list.iloc[-1]
    rs = hist.clock_starts.tail(3).sum() / hist.clock_starts.shift(12).tail(3).sum()
    rp = hist.clock_stops.tail(3).sum() / hist.clock_stops.shift(12).tail(3).sum()
    rem = (hist.waiting_list.diff() - hist.net_growth).tail(6).mean()
    for i in range(1, h + 1):
        m = hist.index[-1] + i
        st_, sp_ = hist.clock_starts.loc[m - 12] * rs, hist.clock_stops.loc[m - 12] * rp
        wl_ = wl_ + st_ - sp_ + rem; out.append((m, st_, sp_, wl_))
    return pd.DataFrame(out, columns=["month", "starts", "stops", "waiting_list"]).set_index("month")

Scope, definitions and remaining checks

The report covers published aggregate consultant-led RTT pathways, not unique people, patient-level clinical priority or a complete capacity model. Missing-month checks do not prove complete source coverage. The stock-flow residual and source-row totals require operational reconciliation. Peer selection does not control case mix. Planning references need policy confirmation, and the March scenario is not a validated prediction. Website checks use committed outputs; they do not rerun unavailable raw files.
