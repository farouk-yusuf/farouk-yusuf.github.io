02 / Research that shaped my approach

The disagreement changed my research.

During my PhD, I tested human and AI coding before applying it to a corpus of 29,182 cleaned social media posts about leaving Nigeria.

The average looked reassuring. The question-level breakdown told a more complicated story.

I investigated disagreements, revised the coding rules and set aside a finer scheme when the pilot proved unstable.

03 / The person behind the projects

How I got here.

What I enjoy most is analysing data. My route here has taken me from studying rainfall and rice yields in Nigeria to interviewing newspaper readers, researching migration and learning how AI can support analysis. Along the way, I moved countries, made lasting friendships and got married.

↓ Follow the chapters, from Nigeria to the next opportunity.

  1. 01
    Before 2020
    The starting point

    Geography gave my curiosity a method.

    I graduated as the best student in my geography programme, with first-class honours and As in all my core statistics courses. My undergraduate research examined how rainfall variability affected rice yields in a Nigerian local government area, using statistical analysis and ArcGIS.

    An early connection between analysing data and understanding people’s livelihoods.

  2. 02
    2020–2021
    A new country

    Chevening brought me to Birmingham.

    A Chevening scholarship brought me from Nigeria to the UK to study an MSc in Conflict, Statebuilding and Development. I made good friends, graduated with a distinction and scored 82 in my dissertation. My supervisor encouraged me to pursue a PhD.

    My dissertation investigated newspaper framing and how audiences interpreted it.

  3. 03
    During my MSc
    Reading between the headlines

    I studied the message and spoke to its audience.

    I analysed how northern and southern newspapers framed conflict involving Fulani herders, then interviewed audiences to investigate framing effects. Among the southern audiences I interviewed, frequent newspaper readers expressed perceptions that aligned with the coverage’s negative portrayals.

    These skills transfer to customer research: examining messages and investigating how people interpret them.

  4. 04
    2022
    Going deeper

    A second master’s, then ESRC-funded research.

    My MA in Social Research deepened my training in qualitative and quantitative methods, research design and tools. I scored 75% in the quantitative module. Research associate work added practical experience, and ESRC funding supported my move into doctoral research.

    A stronger foundation for designing studies and evaluating evidence.

  5. 05
    2023
    Life alongside the work

    I got married.

    A personal milestone alongside my research and the life I was building in the UK.

  6. 06
    During my PhD
    Listening in different places

    Online conversations tell part of the story.

    My PhD examines skilled Nigerians’ aspirations to migrate to the UK. I analyse social media discussions and interview people about their experiences. The sources do not always align: online material offers valuable evidence, while interviews help me explore its context. I taught myself Python during the research.

    Relevant to social listening: understanding the needs, concerns and motivations people express online.

  7. 07
    2025
    Following a new interest

    Exploring language models at Oxford.

    At the University of Oxford’s Spring School in Large Language Models, I learned more about machine learning and AI, including fine-tuning. I also tested human and AI coding in my research, investigating disagreements before trusting the headline results.

    A growing toolkit, with the same interest in checking what the results mean.

  8. 08
    2026 · Now

    I completed a government-funded Data and AI automation bootcamp and spent six months on intensive, self-directed learning. I learned SQL for the council payments project and applied my skills to real UK organisational data. The four projects here show how I investigate business questions and communicate recommendations.
