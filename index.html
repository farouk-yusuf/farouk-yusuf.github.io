01 · Local government

Spend and payment controls: Birmingham City Council

SQL · Python · Excel · Power BI

The task. Produce the monthly spend report for finance, explain this year's variance against last year, and give accounts payable a list of payments to check.

247,785payment records, July 2024 to July 2026 (£3.9bn)
£4.45min 281 possible duplicate payments flagged for checking
213payments to individuals that could be re-identified despite redaction
  • Built a SQL star-schema database and the monthly spend and year-to-date variance report by directorate. Year-to-date spend is 21% above last year, mainly because of the timing of payments on the highways PFI contract.
  • Agency staff cost £101m in 2025/26 and is up 29% year to date, with one agency taking 84% of it.
  • Flagged 281 possible duplicate payments (the same one-off amount to the same supplier within three days) and 260 suppliers held under more than one supplier record.
  • The council redacts individuals' names but still publishes their supplier numbers. Twelve of those numbers also appear with a name, so 213 payments can be re-identified. I pseudonymised them and wrote the issue up for information governance.

02 · Social housing

Tenant satisfaction benchmarking: Platform Housing Group

Python · Excel

The task. Prepare the annual Tenant Satisfaction Measures report for the board: how Platform compares with similar landlords, where to focus, and whether the data can be trusted.

64.6%overall satisfaction, exactly at the median of 17 large housing associations
38%Ombudsman maladministration rate, lowest of 21 Midlands landlords (median 57%)
6 of 12satisfaction measures in the bottom quartile, mostly communication and neighbourhoods
  • Benchmarked all 22 measures against two peer groups. Large landlords score lower across the sector, so they are the fair comparison.
  • Set three priorities by combining Platform's gaps with the measures most closely linked to overall satisfaction: listening and acting on tenants' views, keeping tenants informed, and keeping homes well maintained.
  • Extracted complaint outcomes from 21 Housing Ombudsman PDF reports with a script, and checked that category totals reconcile with the headline figures.

Caught before the board saw it. Non-emergency repairs in target appeared to fall from 92% to 62%. The underlying counts showed the number of repairs recorded had fallen from 90,250 to 13,872 with the same 28-day target, which points to a change in how repairs were counted, not a fall in performance.

03 · Water

Storm overflow performance pack: Severn Trent Water

Python · Excel

The task. Check the annual storm overflow return before it is published, build the year-end performance pack, and send exception lists to the teams who act on them.

2,452overflows validated in the 2025 Environment Agency return
27repeat monitor failures sent to maintenance
13high-spilling overflows with no improvement action, for investment review
  • Combined six years of returns, published in four different formats, into one overflow-level dataset that reconciles with the Environment Agency's own totals.
  • Spills fell 41% in 2025, but rainfall fell 28%. About half the fall is explained by the drier year. Comparing 2025 with 2022, an equally dry year, spills per overflow were 23% lower.
  • Storm tanks at treatment works are 13% of overflows but produced 47% of 2025 spill hours.

04 · NHS

Elective waiting times report: University Hospitals Birmingham

Python · Excel · Power BI

The task. Produce the monthly waiting times report for the trust: progress against national targets, why the waiting list is moving, which specialties hold the long waits, and where the list is heading.

68.6%within 18 weeks in March 2026, against a 65% target
+15%referrals, April to August 2026 against a year earlier; treatments flat
41monthly NHS England extracts processed (3.3 GB)
  • UHB met its March 2026 targets and has the best 18-week performance of seven large Midlands trusts.
  • The waiting list has grown 7.5% since March. Splitting the change into referrals and treatments showed the cause is demand, not falling activity.
  • Back-tested two forecasting models on the last six months. Both missed the turning point, so the March 2027 outlook is presented as a scenario, not a prediction.
