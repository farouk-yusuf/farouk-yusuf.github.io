# Platform Housing Group: tenant satisfaction and complaints, 2024/25

*Benchmarked against a cohort of 17 large housing associations with more than 40,000 homes, including Platform, using Regulator of Social Housing TSM data. Housing Ombudsman reports labelled 2025/26 provide a separate comparison of Platform and 20 Midlands landlords. Full tables are in `Platform_TSM_Benchmarking_Pack.xlsx`; read the [evidence review and outstanding checks](03_Website_Validation_Notes.md) alongside the workbook.*

## Headlines
- **Overall satisfaction is 64.6%, up 2 points and exactly at the large-landlord median.** Large landlords score lower than smaller ones across the sector, so they are the fair comparison group.
- **Platform is in the bottom quartile on six of the twelve satisfaction measures,** all about relationship and neighbourhood: communal areas (-9.1 pts vs median), keeping tenants informed (-7.3), positive contribution to neighbourhoods (-6.0), listening and acting (-4.9), ASB handling (-4.2) and fair treatment (-1.4).
- **Strengths:** building safety compliance at 100% on all five checks, emergency repairs in target (98.2%, top quartile), and complaints answered on time (98% at stage 1, top quartile).
- **Repairs account for the largest number of adverse Ombudsman findings: 23 of the 43 repairs findings.** Across all categories, the extraction records 51 adverse findings out of 137. These counts imply 37.2%, which does not reproduce the recorded 38% headline rate. The other 20 landlords have a median recorded rate of 56.5%; hold any rate/ranking conclusion pending source-report reconciliation.

## Where to focus
Across all landlords, the measures that move most closely with overall satisfaction are home well maintained, listening and acting, repairs, safety and fair treatment. Combining that with Platform's gaps, the priorities are:
1. **Listening and acting on tenants' views** (strong link to overall satisfaction, 4.9 pts below median)
2. **Keeping tenants informed** (7.3 pts below median)
3. **Home well maintained** (strongest link to overall satisfaction, 2.0 pts below median)

## Check before this goes to the board
Three year-on-year movements require reconciliation. Recording changes and genuine service changes are both possible:
- **Non-emergency repairs in target fell from 91.7% to 62.3%,** while the number of repairs counted fell from 90,250 to 13,872 with the same recorded 28-day target. Confirm population and inclusion rules with Repairs before explaining the decline.
- **ASB cases per 1,000 homes rose from 33 to 118** (1,619 to 5,716 cases). Confirm the recording definition.
- **Stage 1 complaints rose from 43 to 73 per 1,000 homes.** May be real or wider recording under the statutory Complaint Handling Code.

## Method notes
Platform's survey was mainly by telephone, the most common method across the published dataset (242 landlords). The primary cohort was selected by size and landlord type, not survey method; survey and tenant-mix differences may still affect comparison. Correlations show association across landlords, not cause. The three priorities are an analyst-selected starting set, not a prediction of intervention benefits. Ombudsman category extraction has unresolved small differences for four other landlords, and Platform's rate/count discrepancy requires source verification.
