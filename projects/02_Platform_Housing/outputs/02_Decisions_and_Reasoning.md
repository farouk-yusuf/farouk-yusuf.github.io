# Decisions and reasoning (interview prep)

**Why large housing associations as the main peer group?** The Midlands median overall satisfaction is 77% but the large-landlord median is 65%: big landlords score lower across the sector. Comparing Platform with small local associations would make it look worse than it is.

**Why check the year-on-year movements?** A 29.4-percentage-point fall in repair timeliness would dominate a board paper. The workbook records an approximately 85% fall in the repairs counted, with the same 28-day target. That raises a comparability question; it does not establish a recording change or exclude genuine deterioration. Reconcile both years with Repairs before explaining the rate.

**Why correlations for 'drivers'?** With one row per landlord, correlation shows which measures rise and fall together across the sector. It isn't proof of cause, so it's used to set priorities, alongside Platform's own gaps.

**Why extract the Ombudsman PDFs?** The Ombudsman only publishes these figures as Power BI PDF reports. I extracted the headline and category tables from 21 reports with a script and checked that category totals reconcile with headline findings (they match within 1-2 findings for four landlords, where a category label couldn't be read).

**Which extraction checks remain open?** The small peer-category differences above remain unresolved. Platform's categories sum to 137 findings and 51 adverse findings, matching its extracted headline counts; however, 51/137 gives 37.2%, not the recorded 38% rate. Check the original PDF, extraction and rate denominator before using the rate or ranking. The repairs category has 43 total findings, of which 23 are adverse; 43 is not Platform's total adverse findings. See the [website evidence review](03_Website_Validation_Notes.md).

**Why only one year of Ombudsman reports?** Earlier years have been removed from the website; each 2025/26 report includes the two previous years' headline rates, which gives the trend.
