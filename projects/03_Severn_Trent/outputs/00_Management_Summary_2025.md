# Storm overflow performance 2025: management summary

*Prepared from the 2025 Environment Agency annual return. Full tables in `Severn_Trent_Overflow_Performance_Pack_2025.xlsx`.*

## Performance
| | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total spills | 62,085 | 36,471 | -41% |
| Spill hours | 454,155 | 200,361 | -56% |
| Overflows with 60+ spills | 312 | 76 | -76% |
| Monitors working ≥90% of year | 91.3% | 96.3% | +5.0 pts |
| Midlands rainfall | 1,000 mm | 717 mm | -28% |

2025 was a much drier year, so these falls overstate underlying improvement. Roughly half the reduction is explained by rainfall; at equal rainfall (2022 vs 2025) spills per overflow are 23% lower.

- **Severn basin** fell further (-52%) than the **Trent basin** (-37%); the Trent basin holds 58 of the 76 high-spilling overflows.
- **Storm tanks at treatment works** are 13% of overflows but produced 47% of 2025 spill hours.

## Return quality checks
All validation checks passed: no duplicate IDs, no impossible values, a reason given for every monitor below 90%. For confirmation before publication: 11 new and 18 removed overflows since the 2024 return, and 2 overflows whose spills rose by 50+ despite the drier year.

## Actions
1. **Maintenance:** 87 monitors worked less than 90% of the year. 27 were also below 90% in 2024: repeat failures to fix first. The main causes are installation/design issues (29), sensor faults (21) and comms faults (18).
2. **Investment / operations:** 76 overflows spilled 60+ times even in a dry year. 13 report no improvement action in 2025 and should be reviewed first.
3. **Reporting:** publish 2025 figures alongside rainfall context, so a wetter 2026 isn't read as deterioration.
